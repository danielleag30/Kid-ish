🎟️ Welcome to New York!

The train doors open and — surprise — you're in New York City. Cue up "Welcome to New York" by Taylor Swift. First stop: dropping bags at the hotel, then straight into the wild. Today's mission: find the animals, find the toys, and see the whole city from 70 floors up, right as the sun goes down.

🦁 Central Park ZooSnow leopards, red pandas, penguins doing penguin things. Grab lunch nearby first.
🍄 Nintendo NY + LEGO StoreBrantley & Preston — this one's for you. Try every Switch demo, build anything, zero rules.

🧾 GROWN-UP INTEL — DAY 1
Red Cap service on arrival: free porter/luggage help at Penn Station — genuinely worth it for 6 people with bags. Best odds of getting one: call Amtrak (800-USA-RAIL) ahead of time or have the conductor radio ahead before you pull into Penn Station, since they primarily meet trains that requested help rather than roaming the platform. Tip $5-10/bag or $10-20/person if used.
Zoo's an 18-min walk from the hotel if you'd rather skip the car entirely. If you do Uber, 6 people means a standard Uber (seats 4) won't cut it — XL or two cars, same math as the Day 4 station problem.

Hotel confirmed: Residence Inn Manhattan/Midtown East, 148 E 48th St. ~10 min from Top of the Rock/Rockefeller Center, ~18 min walk from the zoo (genuinely walkable, Uber optional not required), ~25 min walk from Penn Station.

Check-in is 4pm — you'll arrive around 2pm. This is a bag-check at the bell desk, not full room access. Don't expect to get upstairs until later.

Timing: Lunch at Moynihan Food Hall, Penn Station (~1:30-2pm) → drop bags at hotel bell desk → Uber/subway/walk to the zoo (~2:30-3pm arrival) → Nintendo/LEGO/FAO Schwarz (~4:30-6pm) → Top of the Rock, confirmed reservation 7:10pm (sunset Aug 1 is ~8:13pm, so you'll catch daylight into dusk).

Zoo hours: open 10am-5:30pm Saturdays, last entry 4:30pm. A 2:30-4:30pm visit is genuinely enough time — it's a compact 6.5-acre zoo, most people do it in 1-2 hours.
Option: skip the zoo entirely. If the kids are more excited about the stores, Nintendo/LEGO/FAO Schwarz are the actual top priorities — the zoo is nice but not irreplaceable. Skipping it just means a longer, more relaxed shopping block instead (roughly 4:30/5-7:45pm with a snack break) with dinner shifting to after Top of the Rock instead of before.
Dinner placement is flexible — before or after Top of the Rock, your call in the moment. Before means eating ~6-6:45pm to make the 7:10 reservation comfortably. After means a later dinner ~8:15pm+ once you're down from the observation deck. Neither is locked in — decide based on how hungry/tired everyone is by 6pm.

SUNDAY: LIBERTY, BRIDGES & A SKY RIDE

Aug 2 · Statue Ferry → Battery Park → DUMBO → 9/11 Memorial → Tram → Ice Cream

🗽 Say Hi to Lady Liberty

She's been standing in that harbor since 1886 holding a torch up for everyone who's ever sailed into this city. She has never once sat down. Today you ride a boat right past her — twice — then spend the day at two different playgrounds, two carousels, a floating gondola ride across a river, and end with ice cream. Big day. Worth it.

⛴️ Board the Ferry — Midtown WestRight from West 39th St. No subway required. Ride all the way out toward Staten Island and straight back — that's the part that passes the statue.
🗽 Statue of Liberty, from the water — TWICEOnce on the way out, once on the way back. Best view from the right side of the boat. Wave at her. She's used to it.
🏖️ Battery Playscape + Sea Glass CarouselAn hour on a boat is an hour of pent-up energy. This is where it goes.
🍕 LunchWherever hunger strikes first — Battery Park or once we're over in DUMBO.
🌉 Brooklyn Bridge or Ferry to DUMBOWalk it if legs allow, ferry it if they don't. Both get you there.
🎠 Main St Playground + Pebble Beach + Jane's CarouselThe best playground of the whole trip. This is the big one.
🍦 Snack: Time Out Market (Rooftop)Everyone picks their own food. No group menu negotiations.
🕊️ 9/11 MemorialA short, quiet stop. Brantley and Preston — you'll learn more about this at school soon, and this is a real piece of history. We'll keep it brief and calm.
🍕 Dinner: Little Italy PizzaTwo of the most talked-about pizza slices in the whole city are basically next door to each other. We'll pick one (or both, if everyone's still hungry).
🚡 Roosevelt Island TramA cable car that flies over the East River — the same kind of ride you'd find at a ski resort, except it's the subway.
🍨 Dessert near the tramFrrrozen Hot Chocolate awaits. Last stop before the hotel.
🧾 GROWN-UP INTEL — DAY 2

The ferry route: board the NYC Ferry St. George route at West 39th St, Midtown West. The statue is out in the harbor past the tip of Manhattan, so you have to ride the full route out toward Staten Island and catch the next boat straight back to actually pass her — roughly an hour of sailing, statue twice. Congratulations, you've paid money to look at a large green woman from a boat, twice. Worth it. If you have to get off at St. George, Bake Culture is a bakery literally as you step off the ferry — donuts, pastries, coffee. Kids don't need to know this exists; it's your fallback, not theirs.

Fare is per ride, not a day pass: $4.50/one-way, per person, per boat. Free transfer to a different NYC Ferry route within 120 minutes of activating your ticket. Kids under 44" ride free with an adult. There's also a $15 Unlimited 2-Day Ticket (per person, unlimited rides for 48 hours) — with this many legs in one day, do the math before buying six separate single-ride tickets like a sucker.

Lunch is genuinely flexible — Battery Park if the kids are already losing it, Time Out Market in DUMBO if everyone can hold out for a later, better lunch. No fixed rule here, no one's grading you.

Ferry, Pier 11 to DUMBO: ~4-7 minutes. It's a hop, not a cruise — don't oversell it to the kids as a long boat ride, they will notice the betrayal.

Getting from DUMBO back toward the Memorial: flexible — walk the bridge, ferry, subway, or Uber. The bridge walk itself plus the 10-15 min walk to the Memorial adds up to roughly 20-30 minutes total for kids — factor that in as real walking time, not a quick hop, when deciding whether to walk or take the ferry/subway instead.

Most NYC playgrounds close at dusk and aren't lit at night — sunset's around 8:13pm this time of year. If you're rolling through late, don't count on a playground being usable; it's ambiance at that point, not recreation.

Serendipity 3 (original, 225 E 60th): a few blocks from the tram's Manhattan-side entrance. Reservation not required but strongly recommended — walk-ins do get turned away during busy windows. Open 10am-11pm Sundays.

Dinner, top pick: L'Industrie, Little Italy (197 Grand St) — widely ranked one of the best pizza slices in the US, no reservations, first-come-first-served, they call your name. From here, the 6 train runs direct from Spring St to 59th St (~20 min, zero transfers) — same line covers the whole 9/11 Memorial → dinner → tram chain in one continuous shot, not the zigzag it looks like on a map.

🧾 GROWN-UP INTEL — DAY 4
Train confirmed for ~2pm. Hotel confirmed: Residence Inn Manhattan/Midtown East, 148 E 48th St. From here: NYPL/Bryant Park ~10-11 min walk, Grand Central/Whispering Gallery ~6 min, Penn Station ~25 min walk (or a quick subway/cab).
Complimentary hot breakfast is included at the hotel — worth knowing even though the plan is to keep this loose. It's there if anyone wants it, no reservation, no cost, zero planning required either way.
Rose Reading Room, no tour needed: open for a visiting hour 10-11am (last entry 10:50am) — just walk in. Get there before 10:50am if you want to see it.
Back pocket, not a plan: the Empire State Building (34th/5th) and Macy's (34th/Broadway) both sit directly on the walk from Bryant Park to Penn Station — no detour required. Point out the ESB in passing, duck into Macy's for two minutes if the kids are into it. Neither needs to be announced as a "thing we're doing."
Filler option if the kids are losing it: Grand Central Terminal's Whispering Gallery, near the Oyster Bar, two blocks past Bryant Park. Free, no ticket, no line. Stand in diagonal corners of the arched ceiling and you can hear each other whisper from clear across the room — genuinely startles kids the first time. Also has the celestial-ceiling Main Concourse and a market for a snack. Good five-minutes-of-magic reset button, not a planned stop.
Breakfast is genuinely unplanned — don't build the morning around it. Grab bagels/donuts en route, use the hotel's included breakfast, or eat the protein bars from home and get real food at lunch. The only actual deadline is the 10:50am Reading Room cutoff if that's happening.
Red Cap service on departure: this direction is easier — go straight to the Red Cap desk in the Amtrak waiting room (or the street-level kiosk at 33rd & 8th), tell them your train number and bag count. They'll tag everything, cart it, and take you down by elevator instead of stairs/escalators with 6 people and bags — plus they'll help you find seats together. Tip $5-10/bag or $10-20/person.
A standard Uber seats 4, not 6 — with a 25-min walk to Penn Station, a car is probably worth it this time — plan for an XL or two vehicles, not a last-minute scramble at checkout.
Grown-up victory lap: four kids, four days, zero lost shoes. Nobody will believe you. Take the receipts.
